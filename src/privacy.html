Privacy policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026 · Applies to the DMARC Shield Outlook add-in and this website.

In short

DMARC Shield does not collect, store, sell or transmit your personal data or your email. There is no DMARC Shield server, account, analytics or tracking. The publisher never receives any information about you or your messages.

What the add-in does with your email

When you open DMARC Shield on a message, it asks Outlook for that message's internet headers and sender address. It analyses them in the add-in itself, inside Outlook on your device. The headers, addresses and content are not sent to the publisher or to any third party, and they are not stored: the analysis is discarded when you switch messages or close the pane.

The add-in requests the lowest Outlook permission that can read headers (read item). It cannot read other messages, send mail or change anything in your mailbox.

Optional DNS lookup (off by default)

Some mail providers, notably Microsoft 365, don't record the sender's DMARC policy in the headers. If you click Look up policy or enable automatic lookups in the settings, the add-in asks a public DNS-over-HTTPS resolver for the DMARC record of the sender's domain.

  • What is sent: only DNS names of the form _dmarc.example.com, for the sender's domain and, if no record exists there, its parent domains. No message content, email addresses, subjects or identifiers are sent.
  • To whom: the resolver you choose: Quad9 (default), Cloudflare or Google. As with any internet request, the resolver can see your IP address. Their policies: Quad9, Cloudflare, Google.
  • Storage: results are kept in memory only while the pane is open.

Settings

Your settings (info bar, DNS lookup, resolver, language) are stored with Outlook's roaming settings. This means they are saved in your own mailbox by Microsoft, so they follow you across Outlook apps. The publisher cannot access them.

Microsoft and hosting

  • Like every Outlook add-in, DMARC Shield loads Microsoft's Office JavaScript library (office.js) from Microsoft's servers, and runs inside Outlook. Microsoft's handling of data is described in the Microsoft Privacy Statement.
  • The add-in's files and this website are static files hosted on GitHub Pages. When your Outlook downloads them, GitHub processes your IP address to serve them, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement. The publisher has no access to these logs and adds no analytics.

Technical enforcement

The add-in ships with a strict Content Security Policy. The browser only lets it load code from itself and Microsoft's Office library, and only lets it connect to the three DNS resolvers above. The source code is public, so anyone can verify these statements: github.com/berndverhofstadt/dmarc-shield.

Children

DMARC Shield is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone.

Your rights

Because the publisher holds no personal data about you, there is nothing to access, correct or delete. For data processed by Microsoft, GitHub or your chosen DNS resolver, please contact them directly.

Changes and contact

Changes to this policy are published on this page and in the project's change history. Questions: open an issue at github.com/berndverhofstadt/dmarc-shield/issues.
