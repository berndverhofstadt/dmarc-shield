See at a glance whether an email really comes from who it says.

DMARC Shield is a free, open-source Outlook add-in. It reads the authentication results your own mail server already recorded (DMARC, SPF, DKIM and ARC) and turns them into one clear verdict, right next to the message. Everything is checked inside Outlook: the message and its headers never leave your device.

Three clear results

✓ Verified

The message really comes from the sender's domain, and that domain tells mail servers to reject anyone who fakes it. Mail from inside your own organisation is also verified.

✓ Authenticated

The message really comes from the sender's domain, but the domain doesn't fully block fakes (its DMARC policy is quarantine or none), or the policy isn't known.

✕ Not protected

The sender couldn't be verified, the domain publishes no DMARC policy, or there are signs of forgery, such as faked headers, a mismatched sender or a lookalike domain.

What it checks

DMARC, SPF and DKIM

Shows each result your mail server recorded, with the domains involved, in plain language.

Forwarded mail (ARC)

Mailing lists and forwarders often break SPF and DKIM. When your mail server trusts the forwarder's ARC record, DMARC Shield shows the original result and who forwarded it.

Forged headers

Senders can insert fake authentication headers. DMARC Shield only trusts the one added by your own mail server, and flags conflicting or contradicting headers.

Lookalike domains

Warns about mixed alphabets (pаypal with a Cyrillic “а”), confusable characters, lookalikes of your own domain, and Reply-To addresses that imitate the sender.

Brand logos (BIMI)

Optionally, DMARC Shield shows the sender's brand logo. It's green when the logo is backed by a Verified or Common Mark Certificate, checked in the add-in, and blue when the domain publishes a logo without one. Logos are only shown for mail that passed DMARC with an enforcing policy, so a spoofed message can't borrow a real brand's logo.

Privacy by design

  • No server, no account, no tracking, no analytics. There is no DMARC Shield backend at all.
  • Headers are read and analysed in the add-in, inside Outlook. They are never sent anywhere.
  • Optional: if your mail provider doesn't record the sender's policy (common with Microsoft 365), you can choose to look it up. Only the sender's domain name is sent, to a DNS resolver you choose (Quad9, Cloudflare or Google). This is off by default.
  • Optional brand logo check (BIMI): downloads the logo and certificate from the address the sender publishes in DNS, which can see your IP address. Off by default.
  • A strict Content Security Policy enforces this in the browser. Read the privacy policy for details.

DMARC Shield runs when you open it on a message. Pin the pane (Microsoft 365 work and school accounts) to check every message you select automatically.

Limits

DMARC Shield reports what your mail server recorded; it doesn't re-verify signatures itself. A verified sender proves where a message came from, not that its content is safe. Stay alert for unexpected requests, links and attachments.
