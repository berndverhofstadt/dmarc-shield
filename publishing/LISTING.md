## Summary (≤ 100 chars)

See at a glance if an email really comes from its sender: DMARC, SPF, DKIM, checked on your device.

## Description (≤ 4000 chars)

DMARC Shield shows, right next to the message, whether an email really comes
from the domain it claims, and whether that domain protects itself against
spoofing.

Your mail server already checks every incoming message with DMARC, SPF and
DKIM, but the results are buried in technical headers. DMARC Shield reads
them and turns them into one clear result:

• Verified (green): the message passed DMARC, and the sender's domain tells mail
  servers to reject fakes (p=reject). Mail from inside your own organisation is
  also verified.
• Authenticated (blue): the message passed DMARC, but the domain only
  quarantines or monitors fakes, or its policy is not visible.
• Not protected (red): the sender could not be verified, the domain has no DMARC
  policy, or there are signs of forgery.

What it checks
• DMARC, SPF and DKIM results, with the domains involved, in plain language.
• Forged headers: senders can insert fake authentication results. DMARC Shield
  trusts only the result added by your own mail server, and flags conflicting
  or contradicting headers.
• Forwarded and mailing-list mail: when your mail server trusts the forwarder's
  ARC record, the original result is shown together with the forwarder's name.
• Lookalike domains: international characters that imitate letters (for
  example a Cyrillic "а" in "pаypal.com"), mixed alphabets, lookalikes of your
  own domain, and reply addresses that imitate the sender.
• A short notice in the message's information bar, and an explanation of what
  each result means.

Privacy by design
• Everything is analysed inside Outlook on your device. Message headers and
  content never leave the client. There is no server, account, tracking or
  analytics.
• Optional, off by default: Microsoft 365 doesn't record the sender's DMARC
  policy in the headers. If you choose to, DMARC Shield can look it up in DNS.
  Only the sender's domain name is sent, to the resolver you pick (Quad9,
  Cloudflare or Google).
• Lowest permission needed to read headers ("read item"). Open source under the
  MIT licence.

Requirements and limitations
• Works in Outlook on the web, new Outlook for Windows, classic Outlook for
  Windows (Microsoft 365) and Outlook for Mac, with Microsoft 365 or Outlook.com
  mailboxes.
• Not available on Outlook for iOS and Android: Microsoft doesn't allow mobile
  add-ins to read message headers.
• Not available for mailboxes on Exchange Server (on-premises).
• Outlook runs add-ins when you open them. Pin the pane (Microsoft 365 work and
  school accounts) to check every message you select.
• DMARC Shield reports the results your mail server recorded; it does not
  re-verify signatures. A verified sender proves where a message came from, not
  that its content is safe.

### Nederlands

**Samenvatting:** Zie meteen of een e-mail echt van de afzender komt: DMARC, SPF en DKIM, lokaal gecontroleerd.

**Beschrijving (kort):** DMARC Shield toont naast het bericht of een e-mail echt
van het opgegeven domein komt en of dat domein zich beschermt tegen spoofing:
Geverifieerd (groen), Geauthenticeerd (blauw) of Niet beschermd (rood). Het
controleert DMARC, SPF, DKIM en ARC, herkent vervalste headers en
lookalike-domeinen, en alles gebeurt binnen Outlook op uw apparaat: headers en
inhoud verlaten nooit de client. Optioneel (standaard uit) kan het DMARC-beleid
in DNS worden opgezocht; dan wordt alleen de domeinnaam van de afzender
verstuurd. Niet beschikbaar op Outlook voor iOS en Android, omdat Microsoft
mobiele invoegtoepassingen geen headers laat lezen.
